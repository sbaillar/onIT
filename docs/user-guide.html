onIT
A physical Microsoft Teams busylight for your desk
User Guide · v1.17 · July 2026

1. What is onIT?

onIT is a small round screen for your desk that always shows your Microsoft Teams status — available, in a meeting, presenting — so the people around you know before they interrupt. A menu-bar app on your Mac (or system-tray app on Windows) follows your Teams presence and drives the display over USB or, on the newer device, Bluetooth.

2. Supported devices

onIT supports two Waveshare boards and tells them apart automatically, flashing the right firmware for whichever one you plug in:

DeviceScreenConnection
ESP32-S3 Touch LCD 1.28″1.28″ round LCD, 240×240, touchUSB only
ESP32-S3 Touch AMOLED 1.75″1.75″ round AMOLED, 466×466, touch, comes with a caseBluetooth (preferred) or USB

The AMOLED model can sit anywhere within Bluetooth range on any USB-C power source — a wall charger, a battery bank — no computer connection needed.

3. Installing

macOS

  1. Download onIT-<version>-macos-arm64.pkg from the latest GitHub release and run it. It installs onIT.app and the onitctl command-line tool.
  2. First launch: right-click onIT.app → Open (the package is unsigned). onIT adds itself to login items; a checkbox in the window turns that off.
  3. Open Presence setup… and follow the built-in guide to connect Microsoft Graph (a free Azure app registration and a one-time-code sign-in, about 3 minutes).
  4. Plug in the device. If a firmware update is offered, click Update firmware and leave the cable alone for ~30 seconds.

Windows

Download the windows-amd64.zip, extract, run onIT.exe. Same features, living in the system tray. SmartScreen warns once (More info → Run anyway). The device appears as a COM port automatically. Bluetooth support on Windows is planned but not in this release — use USB there for now.

4. Going wireless (1.75″ AMOLED)

Pairing

  1. Power the device (any USB-C source) and make sure Bluetooth is on for your Mac.
  2. In the onIT menu, choose Pair busylight… and pick your device (onIT-AMOLED-××××).
  3. The device shows a 6-digit passkey full-screen; type it into the macOS pairing dialog.

That’s it — the link is encrypted and bonded to your Mac. onIT reconnects automatically and prefers Bluetooth whenever it’s available, falling back to USB if you plug the cable in. The menu shows which link is live: ⋆ BLE or ⚡ USB.

Out of the box, the device accepts pairing for its first 5 minutes after power-on. After that, put it into pairing mode by holding a finger on the face for 10 seconds — a progress ring fills while you hold; release early to cancel. Pairing mode lasts 2 minutes. Your device only pairs when you ask it to — a passerby can’t claim it while it’s in use.
Re-pairing (new Mac, reflashed device): if the menu shows “pairing lost”, remove the old entry in macOS Bluetooth settings, hold the face for 10 seconds, and pair again.

Manual states, custom text in your chosen colors, and full-screen emoji are set from the menu — manual choices override Teams until you click Auto (Teams). The five custom messages and emojis you use most sit at the top level of the menu.

6. The standalone clock (AMOLED)

When no computer is connected, the wireless device doesn’t go dark — it shows a round analog clock in the onIT style. To keep accurate time on its own, give it your Wi-Fi once: menu → Set up clock Wi-Fi… (only shown while connected over Bluetooth). Credentials travel over the encrypted link, are stored only on the device, and are used solely for network time (NTP) — the clock stays right through power cuts and daylight-saving changes, using your Mac’s time zone.

Without Wi-Fi the clock still works while onIT is connected and for stretches away from the Mac, but it shows a dimmed, handless face after a power loss until it can sync again.

7. Emoji roulette (AMOLED)

A little desk toy: spin a wheel of your favorite emojis and let fate pick one.

  • Standalone: tap the clock (or the current winner) — the screen cycles your emojis fast, slows over about five seconds, and lands on a random one, which stays up.
  • From the menu: Spin the wheel does the same remotely while connected.
  • The winner stays until you spin again — or your computer connects, which returns the device to live Teams mode.

onIT quietly keeps your top emojis (up to 20) synced onto the device in the background, so the spin runs at full speed with no computer attached.

8. Remote presence (Conditional Access workaround)

If your organization only allows sign-in from managed devices (error 53003), sign in on the managed machine and relay presence to the one with the light:

  1. On the light machine: Settings → Accept remote presence (a dialog shows the exact command and a shared token).
  2. On the managed machine: onitctl -login once, then onitctl -forward http://<light-machine>:8125 -token <tok> kept running.

9. Firmware & troubleshooting

  • Firmware updates are bundled with the app and offered automatically per board over USB; Reflash firmware in the menu recovers a confused device.
  • Device not found (USB)? Use a data-capable cable, and run onitctl -ports to list serial ports.
  • Bluetooth not connecting? Check the tray indicator; power-cycle the device (it re-advertises), and see the re-pairing tip in section 4.
  • Clock blank after power loss? It needs one Wi-Fi sync (section 6) or a Bluetooth connection to learn the time again.
  • App updates: Check for updates… in the menu compares against the latest GitHub release and walks you through the install.
